# ASX Investing Basics — Content Review

**Source reviewed:** `ASX Investing Basics.pptx` exported from the local
classroom  
**Review date:** 6 September 2026  
**Scope:** nine-slide experimental sample; text extraction only  
**Release decision:** **revise before presenting as approved educational
content**

The sample is a useful proof that OpenMAIC can quickly create a structured ASX
course and PowerPoint. It is not yet publication-ready. The central issue is
not design; it is that several statements are oversimplified, undated or
potentially stale, and some risk wording could mislead a beginner.

## What worked

- Clear beginner progression: market, terminology, products, analysis, risk,
  tax, implementation and next steps.
- Prominent general-education disclaimer.
- Sensible introduction to bid/ask spreads, diversification, liquidity,
  franking and record keeping.
- A concise nine-slide format that can be reviewed and edited easily.
- The PowerPoint export is small and practical to share.

## Priority corrections

### Slide 2 — What is the ASX?

- Replace “market operator, clearer” with a plain description of ASX's listing,
  trading, clearing and settlement roles.
- Recheck “2,200+ listed companies” and “~$2.5T market cap”. ASX's July 2026
  statistics reported 2,045 listed entities, and its reporting methodology
  changed in 2024. Use the exact metric, date and source rather than a rounded
  legacy number.
- State that normal trading phases and hours have exceptions; link to the
  current ASX trading calendar.
- Describe current CHESS status carefully. ASX says Release 1 of its replacement
  went live in April 2026, while settlement and subregister Release 2 is planned
  for 2029.

### Slide 3 — Terminology

- “CHESS holds your shares electronically” is too loose. Explain the CHESS
  subregister versus issuer-sponsored holdings and the practical meaning of a
  HIN and SRN.
- Franking credits need a warning that individual benefit depends on tax
  circumstances and current law.

### Slide 4 — Investment types

- Do not imply every ETF is diversified or index-tracking. ETFs can be active,
  narrowly concentrated, leveraged, synthetic or exposed to one asset.
- Describe LICs as closed-ended listed investment companies; avoid implying
  every LIC is actively managed in the same way.
- Use the Australian term **A-REIT** and explain that some are diversified while
  others concentrate in one sector or a few assets.
- Verify the current mFund service status directly with ASX before retaining
  this reference. Its availability was not established in this review; absence
  from a navigation menu alone is not proof of closure. Do not publish an
  unverified list of currently available products.

### Slide 5 — Fundamental versus technical analysis

- Present these as different methods with limitations—not a complete decision
  framework. Add valuation uncertainty, data quality, implementation costs and
  the absence of a guaranteed edge.
- “Support and resistance” and “momentum” should not be presented as reliable
  signals without evidence, sample and failure-rate context.

### Slide 6 — Risk management

- Replace “Risk 1–5% per position”. It conflates position allocation with
  capital at risk, and risking 5% on one trade can be aggressive. Teach position
  value, exposure and loss-at-stop separately, while making clear that stops
  can gap or fail to execute at the expected price.
- Replace the “sleep test” with a more objective distinction between risk
  capacity, tolerance and objective-required risk.
- Add drawdown, correlation under stress, counterparty risk, path dependence and
  leverage.

### Slide 7 — Tax

- The 50% CGT discount is not universal. Eligibility depends on taxpayer type,
  residency, asset and holding period; companies do not receive the individual
  discount. Link to current ATO guidance and tell the learner to verify their
  circumstances.
- Superannuation is not one simple tax rate or account type. Keep this at a
  concept level and date any thresholds, caps or rates.

### Slide 8 — Getting started

- Avoid suggesting every broker uses a “100-point check”; describe identity and
  regulatory verification generally.
- Keep `T+2` dated and sourced. ASX's current public page says Australian cash
  equities settle on T+2, while a possible T+1 move remains under consideration
  and is expected only after CHESS Release 2 if adopted.
- Explain order type, brokerage, spread, settlement funding and CHESS
  sponsorship before encouraging a first transaction.

### Slide 9 — Resources

- Lead with ASIC Moneysmart, ASX, ATO and the product's PDS/TMD rather than
  social forums, broker marketing or unsourced finance sites.
- If a social forum is mentioned, explicitly label it as unverified discussion,
  not a research source.
- CFDs and complex derivatives should be separated into an advanced-risk module,
  not presented as an ordinary next step for a beginner.

## Recommended replacement structure

1. The ASX and how ownership/settlement works.
2. Investment vehicles and what can go wrong with each.
3. Diversification beyond holding count.
4. Return after fees, tax and inflation.
5. AUD/USD exposure for international assets.
6. Risk capacity, drawdown and position sizing.
7. Product documents, source quality and claim verification.
8. Worked hypothetical portfolio trade-offs.
9. Quiz, red-team exercise and source checklist.

This structure is now represented by the preinstalled **ASX investing
foundations** classroom starter in the customised app.
